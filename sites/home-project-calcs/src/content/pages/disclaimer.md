## ARTICLE 5. INTERNATIONAL RESIDENTIAL CODE REFERENCES

**5.1 IRC References.** Certain Tools, including those relating to deck joist spacing and span and to retaining wall height thresholds, reference or summarize tables and provisions of the International Residential Code (the "IRC").

**5.2 Edition Referenced.** Unless a Tool states otherwise, the IRC edition referenced on the Site is the 2024 International Residential Code. Local jurisdictions adopt different editions of the IRC, adopt local amendments, or do not adopt the IRC at all, and the edition and amendments in force at your project location may differ from those referenced on the Site. You must therefore check with your local building department to determine the code requirements that apply to your project.

**5.3 Simplified Summaries.** Any summary of IRC tables or provisions on the Site is simplified for general reference, may omit footnotes, exceptions, and conditions, and is not a substitute for the text of the code adopted in your jurisdiction.

**5.4 Span and Spacing Assumptions.** Span, spacing, and similar figures assume specific lumber species, grades, sizes, and moisture conditions; specific live, dead, and snow loads; specific support and connection conditions; and the absence of concentrated loads (such as hot tubs, planters, or roof structures), each as stated or referenced on the relevant page. If your project does not match those assumptions, the figures do not apply.

## ARTICLE 6. STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS AND ENGINEERING

**6.1 Structural Elements.** Decks, retaining walls, and other structural or load-bearing elements can fail and cause serious injury, death, or property damage if improperly designed or built. Such elements should be designed or reviewed by a licensed professional engineer, and should be built in accordance with the code and permit requirements of the authority having jurisdiction.

**6.2 Retaining Walls.** Any retaining wall that exceeds the height at which the applicable code requires engineering design, or that supports a surcharge (such as a driveway, structure, or sloped backfill) or is located near a property line, structure, or slope, should be designed by a licensed professional engineer. The Tools do not perform structural design.

## ARTICLE 7. CALL 811 BEFORE YOU DIG

Before any excavation, including digging for footings, posts, or retaining wall bases, you should contact 811 (https://call811.com) to have underground utility lines marked. Contact 811 at least a few business days before you dig, as required by the law of your state. Striking an underground utility line can cause serious injury, death, service outages, and liability for repair costs.
