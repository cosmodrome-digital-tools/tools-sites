## What it calculates and who it's for

This calculator estimates how much vinyl or fiber-cement siding to order for a house: the area of the four walls, plus the triangular gable ends under a gable roof, plus a waste allowance, rounded up to whole squares. Siding is sold by the square (100 sq ft). It's for homeowners pricing out a re-side, or checking a contractor's material count. It covers the siding panels only; roofing and paint have their own calculators.

## How to use

1. **Measure the house**: the length, the width, and the wall height from the foundation to the eaves. Or switch to entering a wall area you already have.
2. **Count the gable ends** you'll side (2 on a simple gable roof, 0 on a hip roof) and enter the gable height from the eaves to the peak.
3. **Deduct openings only if you want to.** The Vinyl Siding Institute's method leaves windows and doors in as part of the waste allowance.
4. **Enter carton coverage** from the label to get a carton count.
5. **Read the squares to order.**

## The formula

- Walls (sq ft) = 2 × (House length + House width) × Wall height
- Gable ends (sq ft) = Number of gables × (House width × Gable height ÷ 2)
- Area to side (sq ft) = Walls + Gable ends − Openings deducted
- Area with waste (sq ft) = Area to side × (1 + Waste % ÷ 100)
- Squares = Area with waste ÷ 100, **rounded up**
- Cartons = Area with waste ÷ Coverage per carton, **rounded up**

The **10% waste** default and the 100 sq ft square both come from the Vinyl Siding Institute's installation manual, which says to add 10% to all material estimates.

## Worked example

A 40 × 30 ft house with 9 ft walls, two gable ends 7.5 ft high, no openings deducted, 10% waste, cartons of 200 sq ft:

1. Walls: 2 × (40 + 30) × 9 = **1,260 sq ft**
2. Gables: 2 × (30 × 7.5 ÷ 2) = **225 sq ft**
3. Area to side: 1,260 + 225 = **1,485 sq ft**
4. With waste: 1,485 × 1.10 = **1,633.5 sq ft**
5. Squares: 1,633.5 ÷ 100 = 16.34, rounded up to **17 squares**
6. Cartons: 1,633.5 ÷ 200 = 8.17, rounded up to **9 cartons**

## How to read your results

- **Squares to order** is rounded up to a whole square. Siding is often sold in cartons of 1 or 2 squares; enter the coverage from the label to see cartons.
- **Area** shows each part of the estimate so you can check the walls and gables separately.
- Trim, starter strip, J-channel, corner posts, soffit, and fasteners are ordered separately by the linear foot; this calculator doesn't estimate them.

## Assumptions and limits

- The house is a rectangle with walls of one height, and each gable end is a simple triangle across the house width. Dormers, bump-outs, and second-story walls need their own areas added; enter a total wall area instead if that's easier.
- Openings are not deducted by default, following the VSI estimating method. Deducting them gives a tighter number with less spare material.
- The 10% waste comes from the VSI manual for vinyl siding. Fiber-cement and other products have their own instructions; check them.
- Confirm quantities with your supplier and follow the manufacturer's installation instructions and local codes.

## FAQ

### How many squares of siding do I need?

Add up the wall area and gable area in square feet, add 10% for waste, and divide by 100. A 40 × 30 ft house with 9 ft walls and two 7.5 ft gables needs 17 squares.

### How do I measure a house for siding?

Measure each wall's length and the height from the foundation to the eaves. For a gable end, measure the width and the height from the eaves to the peak, and take half of width × height for the triangle.

### Should I subtract windows and doors?

The Vinyl Siding Institute's method doesn't, because the extra covers waste around the openings. Deduct big openings like garage doors if you want a tighter estimate.

### What is a square of siding?

100 sq ft of wall coverage. A carton is often labeled in squares, such as 2 squares (200 sq ft).
