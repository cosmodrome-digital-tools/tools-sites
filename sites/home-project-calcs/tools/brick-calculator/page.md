## What it calculates and who it's for

This calculator estimates bricks and mortar for a brick wall or brick veneer from the wall's face area. It's for homeowners planning a garden wall, planter, or veneer who want the brick count and the mortar volume before they order. It covers clay or concrete *brick* laid in mortar. For stacked segmental retaining wall *block*, use the Retaining Wall Block Calculator.

## How to use

1. **Enter the wall length and height** in feet, and any openings the brick skips.
2. **Choose one or two wythes** (brick-thick layers).
3. **Keep modular brick** or enter your own bricks per square foot and mortar per 1,000 bricks.
4. **Read the bricks to buy and the mortar.** Waste allowances are under Advanced.

## The formula

- Wall face (sq ft) = Length × Height − Openings
- Bricks before waste = Wall face × Bricks per sq ft × Wythes
- Bricks to buy = Bricks before waste × (1 + Brick waste % ÷ 100), **rounded up**
- Mortar before waste (cu ft) = Bricks before waste ÷ 1,000 × Mortar per 1,000 bricks
- Mortar with waste (cu ft) = Mortar before waste × (1 + Mortar waste % ÷ 100)
- Cubic yards = Cubic feet ÷ 27

For **modular brick** (7 5/8 × 2 1/4 × 3 5/8 in) with 3/8 in mortar joints, the Brick Industry Association's Technical Note 10 lists **6.75 bricks per square foot** of wall and **8.1 cu ft of mortar per 1,000 bricks**. The same note says to add at least **5%** to brick and **15 to 25%** to mortar for waste; the calculator uses 5% and 20%.

## Worked example

A garden wall 20 ft long and 4 ft high, one wythe, modular brick, 5% brick waste, 20% mortar waste:

1. Wall face: 20 × 4 = **80 sq ft**
2. Bricks before waste: 80 × 6.75 = **540**
3. Bricks to buy: 540 × 1.05 = 567 → **567 bricks**
4. Mortar before waste: 540 ÷ 1,000 × 8.1 = **4.37 cu ft**
5. Mortar with waste: 4.374 × 1.20 = **5.25 cu ft** (0.194 cu yd)

## How to read your results

- **Bricks to buy** includes the brick waste allowance and is rounded up to a whole brick. Bricks are often sold by the pallet or cube; ask your supplier how many are in one.
- **Mortar with waste** is the volume of mixed mortar. Check the yield printed on the mortar bag you buy to turn it into bags.
- Toggle the mortar between cubic feet and cubic yards at the top of the results.

## Assumptions and limits

- The wall face is a flat rectangle, laid in running bond. Patterns with headers, soldier courses, or decorative bonds use more brick.
- Mortar covers the bed and head joints only. The collar joint between two wythes, grout, and any mortar for a footing are not included.
- The modular figures are for 3/8 in joints. Other brick sizes or joint widths need their own figures from BIA Technical Note 10 or your brick maker.
- It doesn't design the wall. Footings, reinforcement, wall ties, flashing, weep holes, and height limits are set by your local code and, for veneer, the brick maker's instructions.
- Confirm quantities with your supplier before you order.

## FAQ

### How many bricks are in a square foot?

For modular brick with 3/8 in joints, the Brick Industry Association lists 6.75 bricks per square foot of wall face. Other sizes differ; queen and king sizes need fewer bricks per square foot.

### How many bricks do I need for a wall?

Multiply the wall face area by 6.75 for modular brick, then add 5% for waste. A 20 × 4 ft wall is 80 sq ft, so 540 bricks plus 5%, or 567.

### How much mortar do I need per 1,000 bricks?

For modular brick with 3/8 in joints, BIA Technical Note 10 lists 8.1 cu ft per 1,000 bricks before waste. Add 15 to 25% for waste.

### Is this the same as the retaining wall block calculator?

No. Segmental retaining wall block is stacked dry with no mortar and has its own sizes and height limits. This calculator is for brick laid in mortar.
