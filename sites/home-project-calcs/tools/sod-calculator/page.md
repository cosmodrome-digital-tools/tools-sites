## What it calculates and who it's for

This calculator works out how much sod to order for a new lawn: the lawn's square footage, an allowance for cuts, and how many rolls and pallets that is at your supplier's sizes. It's for homeowners and landscapers laying sod over prepared soil. Roll and pallet sizes vary from grower to grower, so both are inputs, not fixed numbers.

## How to use

1. **Measure the lawn as rectangles.** Split curved or L-shaped lawns into one to three rectangles.
2. **Subtract beds, paths, and patios** inside those rectangles.
3. **Enter your supplier's roll and pallet coverage** (ask when you order).
4. **Read the rolls and pallets to order.**

## The formula

- Lawn area (sq ft) = Sum of Length × Width for each rectangle − Beds and paths
- Area to order (sq ft) = Lawn area × (1 + Extra % ÷ 100)
- Rolls = Area to order ÷ Coverage per roll, **rounded up**
- Pallets = Area to order ÷ Coverage per pallet, **rounded up**
- Square yards = Square feet ÷ 9

The **5% extra** follows Mississippi State University Extension's advice to buy about 5 percent more sod than you think you need. The **10 sq ft roll** and **450 sq ft pallet** defaults come from extension guidance (Virginia Tech says a standard roll is about 10 sq ft; pallets run from about 450 to 700 sq ft depending on the grower).

## Worked example

A 30 × 20 ft back lawn, nothing to subtract, 10 sq ft rolls, 450 sq ft pallets, 5% extra:

1. Lawn area: 30 × 20 = **600 sq ft** (66.67 sq yd)
2. Area to order: 600 × 1.05 = **630 sq ft**
3. Rolls: 630 ÷ 10 = **63 rolls**
4. Pallets: 630 ÷ 450 = 1.4, rounded up to **2 pallets**

Two full pallets hold 900 sq ft, so if your supplier sells partial pallets or single rolls, buying 63 rolls is less sod to lay and pay for.

## How to read your results

- **Rolls** and **pallets** are two ways to order the same area. Use whichever your supplier sells, and check whether they sell partial pallets.
- The chart shows the **spare sod on the last pallet** when you round up to full pallets.
- **Area** shows the lawn and order in square feet and square yards (some growers price by the square yard; switch units at the top of the results).

## Assumptions and limits

- Each area is a rectangle. Curves and odd corners are why the extra allowance exists; raise it for lots of curves.
- Sod is perishable. Extension guides advise laying it within about 36 hours of harvest, so order only what you can lay quickly.
- This doesn't estimate topsoil, compost, starter fertilizer, or seed.
- Confirm roll and pallet sizes with your supplier before you order.

## FAQ

### How much sod do I need?

Measure the lawn in square feet (length × width for each rectangle), subtract beds and paths, and add about 5%. A 30 × 20 ft lawn is 600 sq ft, so order about 630 sq ft.

### How many square feet are in a pallet of sod?

It depends on the grower. Mississippi State Extension says a pallet typically holds 450 to 500 sq ft, and Virginia Tech lists 450 to 700 sq ft. Ask your supplier for theirs.

### How many pallets of sod do I need?

Divide the area to order by your supplier's pallet coverage and round up. A 1,000 sq ft lawn needs 1,050 sq ft with 5% extra, which is 3 pallets at 450 or 500 sq ft each.

### How big is a roll of sod?

Virginia Tech Extension says a standard roll covers about 10 sq ft, but lengths and widths vary. Some growers cut larger rolls for machine laying.
