# Handoff: insulation-calculator

## What was built
- Tool slug: insulation-calculator
- Tool name (meta.json "title"): Insulation Calculator
- What it calculates, in one sentence: It looks up the R-value ENERGY STAR recommends adding to the attic for a US climate zone and the existing insulation level, then estimates Owens Corning AttiCat blown-fiberglass bags or EcoTouch batt packages, plus the matching thickness.
- Layout / chart style / accent chosen: stacked layout, line chart, mustard accent `#a16207` (fiberglass-batt color; kept at intake). Stacked + line is not used by any other tool on main. Category is Insulation and Energy. Cream surface `#fffbeb`, ink `#1c1917`, primary result number `#854d0e`. Contrast: `#a16207` on white is 4.92:1, `#a16207` on `#fffbeb` is 4.75:1, `#854d0e` on white is 6.85:1, `#1c1917` on `#fffbeb` is 16.86:1. All pass WCAG AA for normal text.
- SEO title override (Content & SEO, set at intake): `Insulation Calculator - Attic R-Value`; `benefit` matched. With the site name appended by the frame, the full title is `Insulation Calculator - Attic R-Value | Home & Yard Calcs` (57 characters). The site name is not hard-coded in the tool copy; it comes from site config.
- relatedTools (set at intake): `shingle-calculator`, `roof-pitch-calculator`. The generated page drops any related slug whose folder isn't on main, so the Shingle link stays hidden until shingle-calculator merges and then appears on the next build. Drywall is still not built.

## Sources used
List every source in meta.json "sources" (title, URL, date accessed) and what each one supports.
- Recommended Home Insulation R-Values (ENERGY STAR, based on 2021 IECC Table R402.1.3). https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate/identify-problems-you-want-fix/diy-checks-inspections/insulation-r-values — accessed 2026-10-07. The attic columns are headed "Add Insulation to Attic", so each value is the R-value to add. Supports the attic table only: uninsulated / about 3–4 inches. Zone 1 is R-30 / R-25. Zones 2 and 3 are R-49 / R-38. Zones 4 through 8 are R-60 / R-49. The floors and walls columns on that page are not calculated.
- Significant Changes to the 2024 IECC (NAHB). https://www.nahb.org/-/media/NAHB/advocacy/docs/top-priorities/codes/code-adoption/significant-changes-2024-iecc.pdf — accessed 2026-10-07 (added at intake). Supports the Assumptions note that the 2024 IECC lowered prescriptive ceiling insulation to R-38 in zones 2-3 and R-49 in zones 4-8.
- AttiCat PINK Blown-In Insulation product data sheet, publication 10011273-L (Owens Corning, May 2026). https://www.owenscorning.com/dms/10011273 — accessed 2026-10-07. Supports all nine bag rows used here (R-13, 19, 22, 26, 30, 38, 44, 49, 60; all nine since intake), the identical minimum-thickness and minimum-settled-thickness columns, the 27.5 lb bag, and the rule that both bag count and minimum thickness are required for the labeled R-value. Rows used: R-30 is 14.6 bags per 1,000 sq ft and at least 10.75 in (max net coverage 68.5 sq ft/bag). R-38 is 19 bags and 13.5 in (52.6). R-49 is 25 bags and 17 in (39.9). R-60 is 31.5 bags and 20.5 in (31.8).
- EcoTouch PINK FIBERGLAS Insulation manufacturers fact sheet, publication 10017881 (Owens Corning, December 2012). https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/3e/3ed37fbb-73d8-4fee-bee3-2d20e75c4643.pdf — accessed 2026-10-07. Supports batt thickness: R-13 is 3.5 in, R-19 is 6.25 in, R-30 is 9.5 in, R-38 is 12 in. Also supports the editable coverage default of 64.0 sq ft, which is the row for R-38, 12 in thick, 24 in wide, 48 in long, 8 pieces. The same sheet lists other widths (R-38 at 16 in is 42.7 sq ft; 19.25 in is 51.3 sq ft). Those are not separate menu choices.
- Energy-Efficient Home Improvement Credit: Insulation and Air-Sealing Essentials (U.S. Department of Energy; includes the 2021 IECC climate zone map). https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/articles/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit-insulation-and-air-sealing — accessed 2026-10-07. This is the visitor-facing link. The page says to use the map marked Source: 2021 IECC. The ceiling column on the companion DOE insulation article matches the uninsulated attic column used here. The wall column is not used.
- Guide to Determining Climate Zone by County: Building America and IECC 2021 Updates, Figure 4 (PNNL-33270). https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1893981 — accessed 2026-10-07. Figure 4 is the same numbered 2021 IECC map. Table 1 in that report maps Building America color regions (Marine, Hot-Humid, Mixed-Humid, Cold, and so on) onto more than one IECC zone, so those color names are not used as menu choices.

## Decisions made (choice, source)
Data and assumption choices made from the industry standard or the most
sensible option (defaults, formulas, code editions, rounding, waste factors), each with
its source link. Builder's choices kept unless marked "intake".
- Choice: Code edition. Attic levels follow ENERGY STAR's retrofit table ("Add Insulation to Attic"), which is based on the 2021 IECC Table R402.1.3. The 2024 IECC lowered the new-home minimum (R-38 in zones 2-3, R-49 in zones 4-8); the page says so and that local code may ask for less (intake). ENERGY STAR is the federal consumer recommendation for existing attics, which is what this tool covers. | Source: [ENERGY STAR insulation R-values](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate/identify-problems-you-want-fix/diy-checks-inspections/insulation-r-values); [NAHB, Significant Changes to the 2024 IECC](https://www.nahb.org/-/media/NAHB/advocacy/docs/top-priorities/codes/code-adoption/significant-changes-2024-iecc.pdf)
- Choice: The ENERGY STAR number is the R-value to ADD (intake wording fix). Both attic columns are headed "Add Insulation to Attic", so with about 3-4 inches already there, R-49 (zones 4-8) is added on top and the old layer stays. The primary result is labeled "ENERGY STAR R-value to add". This gives the existing-insulation credit the way ENERGY STAR publishes it; no inches-to-R conversion. Default zone is 4A/4B, uninsulated (R-60). Zones 4C, 5, and 6 share one menu row; 7 and 8 share another, as on the table. | Source: [ENERGY STAR insulation R-values](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/seal_insulate/identify-problems-you-want-fix/diy-checks-inspections/insulation-r-values)
- Choice: The climate-zone link is the numbered 2021 IECC map on the DOE insulation article, with Figure 4 of PNNL-33270 as the same map. The Building America color regions and ENERGY STAR window zones are not used. No county lookup. | Source: [DOE insulation article](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/articles/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit-insulation-and-air-sealing) and [PNNL-33270 PDF](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1893981)
- Choice: Blown fiberglass uses all nine published AttiCat rows, R-13 to R-60 (intake; the first pass used only R-30/38/49/60, so zone 1 "3-4 inches" (R-25) bought the R-30 row, 15 bags, instead of the R-26 row, 13 bags). A goal between rows uses the next higher published row; no interpolation. Bag math is `(bags per 1,000 / 1,000) × area`, then optional waste, then round up to a whole bag. Minnesota-only extra inches and bags are not used. | Source: [AttiCat data sheet 10011273, May 2026](https://www.owenscorning.com/dms/10011273)
- Choice: Settled vs installed depth. The result shows the sheet's minimum thickness, which equals its minimum settled thickness on every row, and the page says the depth must still be there after settling. The sheet requires both the bag count and the minimum thickness for the labeled R-value. | Source: [AttiCat data sheet 10011273](https://www.owenscorning.com/dms/10011273)
- Choice: Bag coverage comes from the bags-per-1,000 sq ft column (not the rounded "maximum net coverage" column), because that is the column the sheet tells installers to meet. Counts are for 27.5 lb AttiCat bags only; other brands have their own charts (page says so). | Source: [AttiCat data sheet 10011273](https://www.owenscorning.com/dms/10011273)

The "before rounding" line keeps two decimals. | Source: [AttiCat data sheet 10011273](https://www.owenscorning.com/dms/10011273); the 0–15% pad is a shopping allowance, not a manufacturer waste factor.
